# MICRO empirical validation campaign

Date: 2026-11-18. Status: proposed research and implementation plan; not a frozen
experimental protocol and not evidence that empirical acceptance has been achieved.

See the [MESO companion campaign](MESO_EMPIRICAL_VALIDATION_PLAN.md) for aggregate
traffic dynamics and the [VTRC source assessment](MESO_VTRC_22_R7_REVIEW.md) for
the mesoscopic calibration methodology. Results and acceptance remain mode-specific.

## Objective and claim boundary

Establish whether production HTC MICRO reproduces specified observed traffic
behaviors within predefined tolerances on held-out recordings. Validate three
claims separately: conditional following, signalized queue discharge, and
autonomous traffic evolution inside a bounded road segment or corridor.

“Complete” means completing the declared campaign and reporting all outcomes. It
does not guarantee a passing result or validate every feature of HTC. MESO,
transit, pedestrians, mode choice, routing decisions, emissions, and city-scale
performance require their own evidence. Lane changing, competing junction
movements, and spillback are covered only where explicitly exercised and scored.

## Starting evidence

The fresh retained-data benchmark executed HTC 3.10.0 and SUMO against 28 NGSIM
Lankershim episodes: 21 following and seven queue episodes, with 35 follower
instances. Both engines ran sigma=0 with seed 42 and sigma=0.5 with seeds 42–46.
The primary v2 cohort contains eight following and four queue episodes.

| Primary HTC measurement | Deterministic | Stochastic |
|---|---:|---:|
| Following position RMSE | 1.980 m | 1.912 m |
| Following causal speed RMSE | 0.682 m/s | 0.653 m/s |
| Queue position RMSE | 4.041 m | 3.662 m |
| Queue headway MAE | 0.463 s | 0.425 s |
| Queue crossing coverage | 100% | 100% |

HTC and SUMO have similar errors under these configurations. The run passed 31
targeted Scala tests, five Python tests, and four exact fixed-seed repeat checks.
Those checks establish bounded execution and measurement integrity; they do not
establish independent empirical validity.

The observations have already been inspected, the original ten-following and
five-queue primary recruitment targets were not met, and only the measured head
leader supplies the changing traffic boundary. The replay invokes production
physics directly rather than the full actor runtime. Spacing, collision, and
emergency-braking diagnostics remain part of the evidence.

## Experiment matrix

| Track | Observations supplied as inputs | Predictions evaluated | Intended evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: conditional following | Initial follower state, measured vehicle lengths, causal measured head motion | Follower positions, speeds, gaps, and error growth over fixed horizons | Longitudinal response under an observed leader |
| B: signalized queue | Initial traffic, independently measured signal phases, geometry, upstream arrivals and relevant downstream conditions | Start-up lost time, discharge headways, queue clearance and trajectories | Queue response generated by HTC controls and interactions |
| C: segment/corridor | Geometry, controls, initial traffic, boundary arrivals and declared route inputs | Internal traffic evolution, travel times, queues, flow and congestion dynamics | Autonomous evolution within the observed domain, conditional on external inputs |

For B and C, followers and internal leaders evolve autonomously after
initialization. Do not reset their positions from observations or replay their
interior trajectories. Measured arrivals at the domain boundary are legitimate
inputs, but the resulting claim remains conditional on that supplied demand.
Supplying observed routes does not validate route choice. Keep boundary inputs
and scored interior observations explicitly separated in the data schema.

## Data feasibility gate

Produce an inventory before committing to a site or corridor. For each recording,
retain source identifiers, access conditions, attribution, license, raw hashes,
time coverage, coordinate definitions and known limitations. Audit:

| Requirement | Evidence needed | Consequence if unavailable |
|---|---|---|
| Trajectories and dimensions | Consistent identities, timestamps, units, vehicle lengths and observation coverage | Restrict or reject affected measurements under frozen quality rules |
| Road geometry | Lane alignment, connectivity, stop lines, lengths, speed limits and entry/exit boundaries | Geometry-dependent claims cannot proceed |
| Signal control | Synchronized actual phase intervals, with timing uncertainty recorded | Signalized autonomous validation remains blocked |
| Boundary conditions | Arrival events, initial occupancy and relevant downstream restrictions | Restrict the domain or identify the missing input |
| Temporal coverage | Enough time to observe target regimes, including queue build-up and dissipation where claimed | Restrict regime coverage or select another recording |
| Independence | Recording sessions, vehicle identities and overlap relationships | Cannot demonstrate the intended held-out split |

The retained Lankershim material lacks a trustworthy synchronized signal schedule.
Recover it from independently checked supporting records or video, or select a
different suitable source. Do not infer green timing from the same departures
used as queue-validation outcomes and then describe the result as independent
signalized validation.

NGSIM is a candidate source, not a guarantee that every track is feasible. Audit
other data if needed; availability, licensing and suitability must be verified
before selection. Preserve original trajectories alongside derived data. Freeze
handling of duplicates, missing frames, coordinate projection, smoothing,
derivatives, vehicle identity and platoon consistency. Assess measurement
uncertainty and preprocessing sensitivity using development data. Do not use
future scored follower motion to construct its initialization or simulator input.

## Development, calibration and held-out evaluation

1. Assign all already inspected episodes to development. Use them for debugging,
   sensitivity analysis, measurement audits and diagnosing omitted controls.
2. Define disjoint calibration and final-validation partitions before evaluating
   final predictions. Partition by recording session and vehicle identity, with
   temporal separation where appropriate; do not randomly partition frames.
3. Where feasible, reserve an additional site for a transfer experiment. Distinguish
   same-site held-out performance from performance at a different site.
4. If calibration is used, declare parameter bounds, objective, search budget,
   optimizer, seeds and stopping rule. Fit only on calibration data. Include
   driver heterogeneity only when it is identifiable and supported by the engine.
5. Freeze the executable, configuration, preprocessing and scoring code before
   final evaluation. Report the default configuration and the selected calibrated
   configuration separately, with primary/secondary status decided in advance.
6. Do not tune parameters, exclusions or acceptance thresholds after seeing final
   outcomes. A revised model needs another untouched evaluation partition.

Classify inspection explicitly: metadata and eligibility auditing may be necessary
to establish feasibility, but examining validation trajectories to guide model
changes compromises their role as untouched observations. Record who or what
accessed each partition and for what purpose.

## Protocol and acceptance gates

Write a new versioned protocol after feasibility and development work. This page
does not freeze numerical tolerances, sample counts, or a final dataset. Each
decision below must be resolved before the final run.

| Gate | Required decision/evidence | Outcome if unmet |
|---|---|---|
| Data adequacy | Eligible observations cover declared regimes, independent recording units and a justified sample requirement | Inconclusive data gate |
| Measurement validity | Units, geometry, timing, identities, preprocessing and metric calculations are independently checked | Measurement failure |
| Engine integrity | Complete expected outputs, correct initialization and accounting, reproducible execution, retained failures | Engine failure |
| Absolute predictive adequacy | Prespecified tolerances for each primary metric and regime, with a defined uncertainty-based decision rule | Scientific rejection or inconclusive result, as specified in protocol |
| Comparative skill | Prespecified comparisons against simple baselines and, secondarily, SUMO | Report comparative result separately from absolute adequacy |
| Transfer | Frozen parameters evaluated on a distinct declared site/condition | Restrict the generalization claim if absent or unsuccessful |

Set absolute tolerances from the intended use and measurement uncertainty; do
not copy the existing errors or provisional SUMO-equivalence thresholds. Specify
whether acceptance applies to a mean, upper confidence bound, quantile, coverage,
or a combination, and whether every regime must pass. Beating a baseline or SUMO
alone is insufficient to establish acceptable field accuracy. No universal
position/headway threshold is asserted here.

Determine sample requirements from the desired estimation precision or power,
development variability, recording dependence and practical coverage. Distinguish
numbers of recordings, sites, episodes, unique vehicles and frames. The old
ten/five recruitment targets are historical requirements, not a general sample
size justification for the new campaign. Freeze the recruitment/stopping rule;
do not stop when a favorable result first appears.

## Measurements and uncertainty

| Scale | Measurements to specify in the protocol |
|---|---|
| Vehicle | Position, causal speed and gap errors; bias; tail errors; errors by forecast horizon |
| Queue | Start-up lost time, discharge headways, maximum queue, queue profile and clearance time |
| Segment/corridor | Travel-time distributions, counts/flow profiles, throughput, congestion onset and dissipation |
| Integrity | Missing trajectories, bumper overlaps, failed admissions, collisions as instrumented, unfinished journeys and output coverage |

Declare definitions, units, initialization/warm-up exclusions, station locations,
time bins, horizon treatment and aggregation weights. Apply the same measurement
operator to field and simulated observations. Missing crossings and incomplete
journeys remain failures or censored outcomes under the frozen rule; do not
silently remove them. Attribute simulator diagnostics according to each adapter's
instrumentation; an uninstrumented HTC collision count is not a zero count.

Report free-flow, constrained following, stop-and-go and queue regimes separately
where data support them. Do not let a pooled average conceal an unsuccessful
regime. Preserve both default and calibrated results and all individual seeds.

Use independent recordings or justified recording blocks as statistical units,
with hierarchical/cluster uncertainty methods where needed. Frames, followers
sharing a platoon, repeated vehicles and adjacent episodes are not independent
field replications. Determine simulation seed counts with a predefined precision
rule; seeds characterize model randomness and cannot replace independent field
observations. Predeclare primary endpoints and how multiple comparisons affect
acceptance; label exploratory diagnostics accordingly.

## Production runtime implementation

Retain direct `MicroFieldReplay` as a component diagnostic. Add native scenarios
that execute through the production Car, Link, Node and TrafficSignal actors for
the supported track and configuration. Check admission, physical clocks,
cross-link leader visibility, geometry, signal intervals, distance conservation,
journey completion and reporting before using field errors to diagnose physics.

Compare identical supported inputs and seeds across the required runtime policies
and layouts. Policy parity is a separate engineering gate; it does not substitute
for field agreement. Validate any conversion/import pipeline and retain its exact
commands and outputs. Unsupported movements or models remain explicit exclusions
and limit the claim; enabling them requires implementation and new evidence.

## Milestones and deliverables

| Milestone | Deliverable | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|
| M1: scope and feasibility | Claim matrix, recording inventory, input availability and independence audit | Each track has adequate inputs or an explicit unresolved blocker |
| M2: development infrastructure | Audited importer, measurements, actor scenarios and baseline implementations | Engineering checks pass on development fixtures; failures are diagnosable |
| M3: calibration and design | Calibration record, sample/precision plan, tolerances and frozen new protocol | All evaluation decisions resolved before final prediction assessment |
| M4: component and queue evaluation | Held-out A/B predictions, metrics, intervals and gate outcomes | Complete evidence retained, whether the outcome passes or fails |
| M5: autonomous segment evaluation | Held-out C traffic evolution and aggregate measurements | Declared domain evaluated without internal trajectory forcing |
| M6: transfer and reporting | Separate transfer results, reproducible bundle and claim/limitation matrix | Every declared gate has an explicit outcome and traceable evidence |

Start with M1 and M2 for following/queue validation, while auditing one candidate
road segment for track C. Do not promise corridor completion until synchronized
controls, boundary conditions and geometry are available. Milestones are dependent
work packages, not calendar estimates or permission requirements.

## Evidence package and failure policy

Archive the frozen protocol; source and partition manifests; hashes and selection
seals; raw and processed observations; preprocessing/calibration code; executable
and dependency versions; configuration; scenarios; exact commands; predictions;
logs; per-recording/episode/vehicle/seed metrics; uncertainty results; figures;
gate outcomes; and attribution/license records. Label every value as measured,
derived, supplied, calibrated, or assumed where that distinction affects claims.

Distinguish data inadequacy, measurement failure, engine failure and scientific
rejection. Retain interrupted and unsuccessful runs. If a final run exposes a
software error, preserve it and document the repair and its effect. Do not
silently replace a failed run. If the repair or subsequent choices use inspected
outcomes to improve the model, move those observations to development and use
fresh held-out evidence for the revised claim.

The final statement must identify the validated behaviors, domain, conditions,
data partition, tolerances and remaining exclusions. A completed campaign can
produce a valid negative or inconclusive scientific result.
